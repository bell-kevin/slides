The problem

A failed test isn't the end of the story.

Utah requires an emissions inspection in five counties. You may test in any of the five. You register where you live.

  • 01
    It gets registered anyway.
    Failed in Davis. Then registered in Davis, with no new passing emission test.
  • 02
    It moves, on paper.
    Fails in Davis, then renews under a Tooele ZIP, where no test is required, while every test it ever took was in Davis.
  • 03
    It simply disappears.
    A failed emission test, and then nothing. Rarer than it looks, once central and online renewals are accounted for.
Why it stayed invisible

Inspection data and registration data live in separate systems. A DMV row with no test attached reads 9999-12-31, which is not the same thing as never tested.

The solution

Join the records. Then explain the verdict.

One database keyed on VIN, holding both feeds. A transparent rule engine scores every flagged vehicle, and every point it earns maps to a sentence a person can read.

A rubric, not a model

An analyst can always answer "why is this one flagged?" with a concrete sentence naming the test county, the ZIP, the registration place, the dates and the station. Never "the model says so." Every factor below is auditable in one file, and tunable.

The scoring rubric
+40Registered after a fail, with no later passing test
+30ZIP moved to a non-I/M county within 90 days of a failed test
+25OBD verdict was FAIL on a test recorded as a pass
+25Check-engine light commanded on, but the test still passed
+20Catalyst monitor not ready (codes cleared before the test)
+15Failed here, then passed at a station with a high flag rate
+10Duplicate or missing certificate number
+10Repeated fail/pass cycles on the same VIN
